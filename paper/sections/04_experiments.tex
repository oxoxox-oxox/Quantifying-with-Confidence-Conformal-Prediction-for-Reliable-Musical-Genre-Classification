\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

\textbf{GTZAN}~\cite{tzanetakis2002gtzan} (999 tracks, 10 genres) and
\textbf{FMA Small}~\cite{defferrard2017fma} (7,994 tracks, 8 genres). Embeddings
are $\ell_2$-normalized 1024-dim vectors from MERT-v1-330M. We use stratified
5-fold 60/20/20 splits; the Linear Probe is trained for 100 epochs (Adam,
$\text{lr}=10^{-3}$, $\text{weight\_decay}=10^{-2}$). We evaluate at $\alpha
\in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.10\}$.

\subsection{Embedding Quality}

On GTZAN, MERT embeddings achieve intra-class cosine similarity $0.917$ vs.\
inter-class $0.873$ ($\Delta = 0.044$). Classical and metal are well-separated;
rock and country overlap, consistent with perceptual ambiguity. On FMA,
separation is substantially weaker ($\Delta = 0.026$), reflecting more entangled
genre categories.

\textbf{LP-Cos-CP} achieves coverage tightly matching nominal targets on both
datasets. At the default $\alpha = 0.05$ setting (95\% confidence), the system
returns 3--4 genres on GTZAN and 5 on FMA; at $\alpha = 0.10$, sets shrink to
2--3 genres on GTZAN (4 on FMA), meaning a user typically inspects only a few
candidates rather than the full label space. Learned prototypes reduce set sizes
by 55\% at $\alpha = 0.10$ compared to centroid-based CP (from 5.40 to 2.42 on
GTZAN). Set size distributions (Figure~\ref{fig:set_size_boxplot}) confirm
compact sets predominate, with empty sets rare ($<1\%$).

\textbf{Bootstrap} produces the tightest sets (2.13 on GTZAN at $\alpha=0.10$)
but marginally undercovers at $\alpha = 0.10$ ($0.899$ vs.\ nominal $0.90$),
breaking the system's confidence promise without user-visible warning. Its
$1{,}000\times$ calibration cost is impractical for interactive settings.

\textbf{Gaussian} catastrophically overcovers: at $\alpha = 0.01$ on GTZAN it
returns all 10 genres (9.96) with $100\%$ coverage, providing zero diagnostic
value. The normality assumption interacts unpredictably with score distribution
shape, producing interface behavior that fails in ways users cannot anticipate.

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Robustness}

Despite FMA's weaker embedding separability ($\Delta = 0.026$), CP's
distribution-free guarantee ensures valid coverage. Set sizes remain
proportionally larger on FMA ($4.08$--$6.85$ for $K=8$ vs.\ $2.42$--$6.13$ for
$K=10$ on GTZAN), reflecting genuine stylistic overlap in the FMA
taxonomy---feedback that itself informs users to expect broader prediction sets
with this dataset.

In summary, learned prototypes substantially improve the user experience by
shrinking prediction sets, the $\alpha$ parameter functions as an effective
trust slider, and distribution-free calibration is a practical necessity for
predictable interface behavior.
