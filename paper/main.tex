\begin{abstract}
When a music streaming service recommends a track as ``jazz'', or a producer
filters AI-generated music by genre, how should a user decide whether to trust
that decision? Current AI music tools produce a single label with no indication
of its reliability, forcing users to either blindly accept or manually verify
every suggestion. We introduce LP-Cos-CP, a framework that augments any
embedding-based music classifier with \emph{prediction sets}---a small,
guaranteed-reliable set of candidate genres for each track, accompanied by a
per-sample confidence signal that requires no statistical training to interpret.
Our method extracts features from MERT-v1-330M, a large-scale self-supervised
music model, learns discriminative genre prototypes via a lightweight linear
classifier, and applies conformal prediction to produce prediction sets with
formal coverage guarantees.
On the GTZAN dataset, LP-Cos-CP narrows predictions to 2--3 genres at 90\% confidence.
On the more challenging FMA Small dataset, where base Top-1 accuracy is only 53.5\%, the prediction set naturally expands to ${\sim}4.1$ genres to robustly maintain the 90\% coverage guarantee, demonstrating its adaptive uncertainty quantification.
Furthermore, our learned prototypes reduce set sizes by up to 55\% compared to centroid-based alternatives.
The significance level $\alpha$ functions as an explicit user-facing trust slider, enabling practitioners to
trade off between cautious mode (more genres, higher confidence) and
exploratory mode (fewer genres, lower confidence). Our results demonstrate that
conformal prediction can serve as a practical interface layer between AI music
models and their human users, providing actionable uncertainty information
without sacrificing statistical rigor.

\keywords{Conformal prediction \and music genre classification \and human-AI interaction \and interpretable confidence \and self-supervised learning.}
\end{abstract}
